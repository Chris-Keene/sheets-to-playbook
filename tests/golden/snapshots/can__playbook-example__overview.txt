The challenges

Each table picked one challenge and worked through what has helped, what hasn’t, and what to try next. Where two tables picked the same challenge, their work is combined. Open any challenge to see the full detail.

LEADERSHIP SUPPORT FADES

Keeping leadership support when sponsors move on

Support often rests on one senior sponsor. When they change role, leave or pick up a new priority, accessibility drops off the agenda and the work starts again from scratch.

Tables 1 and 8 · 3 actions to try

Explore this challenge

HARD TO PROVE IMPACT

Proving impact in terms leaders care about

Leaders ask for numbers, but the changes that matter most, like fewer barriers for customers and more confident teams, are hard to count. Issue counts can even go up when things are improving, because more people are finally looking.

Tables 3 and 9 · 3 actions to try

Explore this challenge

CHAMPIONS BURNING OUT

Stopping champions burning out

Champions usually do this on top of their day job. The most committed people take on the most, nobody sees the extra hours, and they quietly step back.

Tables 4 and 7 · 3 actions to try

Explore this challenge

SEEN AS SOMEONE ELSE'S JOB

Getting product teams to own accessibility

Accessibility gets treated as a specialist's job or a check at the end. Product teams agree it matters, then it loses to deadlines every sprint.

Tables 6 and 10 · 3 actions to try

Explore this challenge

NO BUDGET OR HEADCOUNT

Securing budget before there's a crisis

Without a line in the budget, accessibility runs on goodwill and spare time. Money tends to appear after a complaint or a legal letter, then disappears once the fix is in.

Tables 5 and 12 · 3 actions to try

Explore this challenge

NETWORKS LOSE MOMENTUM

Reviving a champions network that has stalled

Networks often start with energy and a launch event, then attendance drops. A year later there are 200 names on the list and five people turning up.

Table 2 · 2 actions to try

Explore this challenge

BELIEF A TOOL OR AI WILL FIX IT

When leaders think a tool or AI has solved it

When a new tool or AI assistant arrives, leaders can assume accessibility is now handled. Manual testing and testing with disabled people are the first things cut.

Table 11 · 2 actions to try

Explore this challenge
