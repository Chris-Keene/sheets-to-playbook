SEEN AS SOMEONE ELSE'S JOB

Getting product teams to own accessibility
Worked on at table 4
E-commerce
Travel
Banking

Accessibility gets treated as a specialist's job or a check at the end. Product teams agree it matters, then it loses to deadlines every sprint.

Everyone agreed in the meeting. Then sprint planning happened.

The day a product manager watched a customer fail to check out, it stopped being our backlog and became theirs.

What’s been tried
Helped
Accessibility acceptance criteria in the definition of done, written with the teams.
Product managers sitting in on research sessions with disabled participants.
Automated checks in the pipeline, so basic issues block a merge instead of a release.
Got in the way
A central team fixing issues for product teams. It made the problem someone else's.
Training only developers, not designers or product managers.
What we learned
Ownership follows exposure: people who watch a real user struggle start owning the fix.

Co-write acceptance criteria
What to do
Work with one team to write accessibility acceptance criteria for their next feature.
What you need
A willing product manager and 30 minutes in refinement.
How it sticks
Turn the criteria into a template the team reuses, then take it to the next team.
Good for
Just starting
Growing
Get product managers in the room
What to do
Invite product managers to watch a session with a disabled user.
What you need
Research budget or a partner organisation, and participant consent.
How it sticks
Put one session a quarter in the product calendar.
Good for
Just starting
Growing
Established
Catch the basics automatically
What to do
Run automated accessibility checks in the build pipeline so basic issues show up before review.
What you need
An engineer to set it up and agreement on which failures block.
How it sticks
Share trends with team leads monthly, and keep manual and user testing for what automation can't catch.
