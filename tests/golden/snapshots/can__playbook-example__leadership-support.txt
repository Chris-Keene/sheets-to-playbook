LEADERSHIP SUPPORT FADES

Keeping leadership support when sponsors move on
Worked on at tables 1 and 8
Banking
Central government
Retail

Support often rests on one senior sponsor. When they change role, leave or pick up a new priority, accessibility drops off the agenda and the work starts again from scratch.

We had a brilliant sponsor for two years. She left on a Friday and by Monday we were nobody's problem.

Leaders don't need convincing that it matters. They need convincing it's theirs.

What’s been tried
Helped
Two sponsors, one from the business and one from technology, so one leaving didn't stop everything.
Adding accessibility to the existing quarterly risk report instead of a separate update nobody reads.
A short monthly note to the exec team with one win, one risk and one ask.
Got in the way
A big launch event with the CEO and no follow-up plan. The energy was gone within a quarter.
Relying on legal risk alone. It got attention until the next audit passed.
What we learned
Write the sponsor role down: what they own, how often they hear from you, and who takes over if they move.

Showing all 3 actions.

Name a deputy sponsor now
What to do
Ask your sponsor to nominate a deputy at the same level before anything changes.
What you need
Fifteen minutes with your sponsor and a one-page description of the sponsor role.
How it sticks
Review the pair every six months, and add the handover to your sponsor's own exit checklist.
Good for
Growing
Established
Put accessibility in a report leaders already read
What to do
Add a standing line to an existing report, such as risk, quality or ESG.
What you need
The report owner's agreement and two or three measures you can update each quarter.
How it sticks
Once it's a standing line, a missing update gets noticed.
Good for
Just starting
Growing
Established
Send a one-win, one-risk, one-ask note
What to do
A monthly note of five lines or fewer to your sponsor and their peers.
What you need
A shared template and 30 minutes a month.
How it sticks
Keep the format fixed so it takes no thought to write or to read.
